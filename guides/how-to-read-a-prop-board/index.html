How to Read a Prop Board

A 10-minute daily routine

By the LEGLOCK team · Last updated November 7, 2026

There are two ways to use a prop board. The first is to scroll until a big percentage grabs your eye and run with it. The second is a short routine that takes about ten minutes and catches most of the ways a big percentage can mislead you. This guide is the second way — the exact order we’d suggest working through LEGLOCK’s board each day.

Step 1: Start with who isn’t playing (2 minutes)

Open the Injuries page before you look at a single prop. Absences move everything: when a starter sits, his minutes, shots and touches get redistributed to teammates whose averages were built with him on the floor. A backup’s hit rate over the last ten games may badly understate his role tonight, and a star’s rate may be built on games where a co-star was healthy. Note the big outs, and treat every prop in those games as “context changed — dig deeper.” Also scan the minutes watch: a player whose last three games came with far fewer minutes than his season average is a player whose role is shrinking, whatever his percentages say.

Step 2: Scan for agreement, not just size (3 minutes)

On the Workstation or Discover board, don’t sort by the single biggest number. Look for props where the L5, L10 and L20 windows roughly agree — the trend that survives all three is the trend most likely to be a real property of the player rather than a lucky week. When the windows disagree sharply, that prop isn’t rejected; it’s flagged. Disagreement means something changed, and changed situations are where both the best and worst bets live. Either way, you’ve stopped reading percentages and started reading stories, which is the upgrade this routine exists to make. (The hit rates guide explains the windows in full.)

Step 3: Open the game log (3 minutes)

For every prop still on your list, open the player’s game log. You’re looking for three things. First, shape: is the rate built on steady games or one explosion? Eight games of 26–30 points is a different animal from seven games of 18 and one of 55, even if the counts match. Second, opposition: use the opponent split to see how the player has done against tonight’s team or similar defences. Third, setting: check the home/away split if tonight’s game is on the road, and glance at the minutes column — consistent minutes are the foundation under every stat a player produces.

Step 4: Step the line to your book’s line (1 minute)

LEGLOCK’s lines are research lines, set from recent averages — your book’s line will differ, sometimes by enough to flip the story. Step the line on each surviving prop to match the number you’d actually be betting, and watch the hit rates recompute. Some props fall apart at the real number; occasionally one gets stronger. Only the stepped version means anything for a real decision, which is why this step comes before any conclusion and not after. Our research lines guide explains the gap in full.

Step 5: Decide like a researcher (1 minute)

Finish by saying, out loud if it helps, what would have to be true for each remaining prop to miss: the player’s minutes get cut, the game turns into a blowout, the matchup clamps him. If those things feel more likely than the board implies, pass — passing is a decision, and on most nights it’s the most common correct one. And however strong a prop looks after all five steps, keep the stake inside the budget you set before you opened the site. The routine ends there on purpose: the last step is never “bet bigger because the numbers looked good.”

The routine on one line

Injuries first, windows second, log third, your line fourth, judgement last. Ten minutes, five steps, and you’ll know more about your card than the percentage-scrollers know about theirs — including the most valuable thing of all: which props to leave alone.
